# Annex C — Policy analysis: the "vacancy" (leegstand) limitation

**Complaint:** Wezenstraat 5, 1781 GJ Den Helder · **Complainant:** Mrs Patricia Quinche Guerra
Policy 5410V043736 (Avéro Achmea, via Van Loon / Söderberg & Partners Volmacht) — package 98284786-02 — claim CVBS2632111

## 1. Purpose of this section

Van Loon rejected the water damage claim on one ground only: that the building was vacant ("leegstand") and that, during vacancy, only fire and storm are covered (C2.1, 2 September 2026; C2.3, 11 September 2026).

This section reviews the documents Van Loon itself relied on: the policy schedule and the policy conditions. It sets out what they actually say about vacancy, and what they do not say.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 The limitation exists only as one undefined line on the policy schedule, not in the policy conditions

I do not dispute that the policy schedule contains a vacancy line. Page 2 of C4.1, under "Bijzonderheden", reads:

> "Tijdens de leegstand van het pand is er uitslu brand-/ stormdekking."
> (During the vacancy of the property, there is only fire and storm cover.)

My point is that this one line is the only basis for the limitation. It is not developed or supported anywhere in the policy conditions (VBB 2025-01, C5.1):

- Article 1 ("Begripsomschrijvingen", definitions) defines 31 terms. "Leegstand" is not one of them.
- No article of the conditions limits cover to fire and storm while a building is vacant.
- No provision states how or when vacancy is determined. There is no minimum period, no minimum level of use and no inspection or preventive requirement.
- The words "leegstand" and "leeg staan" appear only three times in the 21 pages: in Article 8.3, Article 9 and Article 11.2.2.3. None of them restricts the insured perils (see 3.2 and 3.3).

The schedule line itself:

- does not refer to any article or clause;
- does not define "leegstand" or say when it begins or ends. "Tijdens de leegstand" (during the vacancy) suggests a period, but the policy never says how that period is measured;
- is incomplete: the word "uitslu[itend]" is cut off in the document.

I was therefore unable to understand what the limitation meant in practice. That is why I asked Van Loon for a written definition on 1 July 2026 (see 3.3). The answer Van Loon gave me was that, after 3 months of vacancy, damage other than fire and storm remains covered.

### 3.2 Article 8 supports my position: the insurer knew the property was vacant from the start

Article 8 ("Bekendheid en risicowijziging", C5.1 page 12) deals with what the insurer knows and with changes in the risk.

- Article 8.2: "Verzekeraar is bekend met de ligging, bouwaard, inrichting en gebruik van de gevaarsobjecten bij aanvang van de overeenkomst alsmede met de belendingen." That is, the insurer is aware of the use of the insured property at the start of the contract.
- Article 8.3 lists "geheel of gedeeltelijke leegstand" (full or partial vacancy) as an increase in risk that must be reported within thirty days. Article 8.3 also sets out the consequences if such a change is not reported.

Article 8 therefore treats vacancy only as a change in risk that must be notified. In my case there was no such change:

- The policy schedule itself describes the property as "Leegstaand Winkelpand" (vacant shop unit) under "Bestemming" (C4.1, page 2).
- The insurer and the intermediary therefore knew of the vacancy and accepted the risk on that basis (Article 8.2).
- There was no unreported change of risk. Article 8.3 cannot be used against me.

Article 8 does not define vacancy, and it does not limit cover to fire and storm.

### 3.3 Article 9 does not say what Van Loon told me

On 1 July 2026 I asked Van Loon for the full written definition of "leegstand" that applies to my policy. The policy schedule only referred me to a website.

After a phone call, I recorded Van Loon's answer in my email of 2 July 2026: "The meaning 'leegstand' is on Art 8 / Art 9. After 3 months of leegstand there is not coverage for fire and storm. Other damages are still covered." On 7 July 2026 Jordi van Rheenen (Van Loon) confirmed in writing: "I can confirm you that your response to A and B is wright."

On 9 July 2026 he confirmed again that after 3 months other damages remain covered besides fire and storm. He also answered "YES" to the question whether clause V5069 (owner's interest: floors, kitchens, sanitary fittings, up to EUR 5,000) applies to me as owner of the individual unit. I asked him to send me Articles 8 and 9; they were not attached to his answers.

What the two articles actually say:

- **Article 9 ("Voortaxatie", pre-valuation, C5.1 page 13):** an agreed valuation of the building loses its validity if "het gebouw staat of zal voor langer dan 3 maanden leeg staan". This rule concerns only the validity of a pre-valuation. It says nothing about which perils are covered, and nothing about fire and storm.
- **Article 11.2.2.3 (C5.1 page 15):** the third mention. It uses the sale value as the basis for compensation if the building was vacant for more than 9 months. This is again a valuation rule, not a limitation of perils.
- **Article 8:** see 3.2.

Neither Article 8 nor Article 9 defines vacancy. Neither limits cover to fire and storm. Van Loon referred me to these articles as the source of the definition, but the definition is not there.

### 3.4 Contradiction with Van Loon's letter of 11 September 2026 (C2.3)

In its final answer to my complaint, Van Loon (W. van Seggelen) wrote that I had received the policy conditions and clauses, and that:

> "Hierin staat duidelijk vermeld dat bij leegstand een beperkte dekking geldt en dat waterschade niet is meeverzekerd."
> (These clearly state that limited cover applies in case of vacancy and that water damage is not insured.)

This statement is not supported by the documents:

- The VBB 2025-01 conditions contain no provision limiting cover during vacancy and no exclusion of water damage for vacant buildings.
- The only clause on the policy schedule, V5069, concerns the owner's interest of individual unit owners. It does not mention vacancy.
- The only reference to a fire and storm limitation is the incomplete line under "Bijzonderheden", which refers to no article.

### 3.5 Other inconsistencies in the policy schedule (C4.1, page 2)

- **Type of property:** "Type bedrijfspand" is stated as "Woonpand" (residential building), while "Bestemming" is "Leegstaand Winkelpand" (vacant shop unit).
- **Cover and premium:** the cover purchased is "Gebouw uitgebreid" (extended building cover), with a yearly premium of EUR 729.22, plus glass cover at EUR 50.24. On Van Loon's own reading, the extended cover could never respond while the unit was vacant, which was the known situation from the start.

### 3.6 The water damage peril has no vacancy exclusion; the conditions restrict only vandalism for unused buildings

- **Article 3.8 ("Water, stoom, neerslag, blusmiddel", C5.1 page 5):** covers water that flows unexpectedly as a result of a breakage, frost, blockage or sudden defect in supply and discharge pipes, and in the installations and appliances connected to them. This is exactly what happened here: a water valve broke. The only exclusions listed are backflow of sewage, moisture penetrating walls, floors or cellars, and construction defects. Vacancy is not among them.
- **Article 3.14 ("Vandalisme", C5.1 page 6):** this is the only peril the conditions restrict for buildings that are not in use: "Deze dekking geldt niet voor gebouwen of gedeelten daarvan die buiten gebruik zijn gesteld." (This cover does not apply to buildings, or parts of them, that have been taken out of use.)

When the insurer wanted to restrict a peril for unused buildings, it did so expressly, and it did so only for vandalism. There is no equivalent wording for water damage.

### 3.7 Van Loon's own written confirmation is consistent with the conditions

**I asked through the proper channel.** On 1 July 2026, before any decision on my claim, I asked Van Loon in writing what my cover included and what "leegstand" meant. Van Loon acts on behalf of the insurer, Avéro Achmea. It decided who would answer me.

**Van Loon's specialist answered twice, in writing, citing the conditions.** Jordi van Rheenen, of Van Loon's business insurance department (zakelijk@van-loon.nl), replied on 7 and 9 July 2026:

- On 7 July 2026 he confirmed my written record of Van Loon's answer (see 3.3): that the meaning of "leegstand" is found in Articles 8 and 9 of the conditions, and that after 3 months of vacancy damage other than fire and storm remains covered.
- On 9 July 2026 he confirmed again that, after 3 months, other damage remains covered besides fire and storm.
- He answered "YES" to the question whether clause V5069 (owner's interest, including floors and kitchens) applies to me as owner of the individual unit.

**This confirmation matches the text of the conditions.** As shown in 3.3 and 3.6:

- Articles 8 and 9 do not limit the insured perils.
- Article 3.8 covers water escaping from broken pipes and connected installations, with no vacancy exclusion.
- The only peril restricted for unused buildings is vandalism (Article 3.14).

Whatever the internal reasoning, Van Loon's written answer was consistent with its own policy conditions.

**The clause is ambiguous even to Van Loon.** The schedule line says that during vacancy there is only fire and storm cover. Van Loon's written answer said the opposite: after 3 months, fire and storm are not covered, while other damage remains covered. If the intermediary's own specialist reads the clause in the opposite way, a policyholder cannot be expected to understand it as Van Loon now applies it. Any ambiguity in a clause drafted by the insurer should be resolved in favour of the policyholder (see section 4).

**I was entitled to rely on this answer.** I asked a precise question about cover, through Van Loon's official channel, and received a clear written answer. I relied on it, which is protected under Articles 3:35 and 3:61 of the Dutch Civil Code.

**Van Loon's objection does not change this.** In its letter of 11 September 2026 (C2.3), Van Loon states that my contacts with its "commerciële collega" are separate from the assessment of the claim, because that colleague was not involved in claim handling. This does not answer the point:

- My question was precisely about what the policy covered.
- It was asked and answered before any decision on the claim.
- The answer came from Van Loon itself, the same company that later rejected cover.

An intermediary cannot confirm cover in writing through one department and then deny it through another, while relying on a clause that it does not interpret consistently itself.

## 4. Conclusion

1. The rejection rests on a single, incomplete line on the policy schedule ("Tijdens de leegstand van het pand is er uitslu brand-/ stormdekking"). That line refers to no article, and "leegstand" is not defined anywhere. The policy conditions contain no article that limits cover to fire and storm during vacancy.
2. Van Loon itself directed me to Articles 8 and 9 for the meaning of "leegstand". Those articles contain no definition and no limitation of perils. Article 8.2 in fact confirms that the insurer knew the use of the property at the start of the contract, and the schedule records it as vacant.
3. The water peril (Article 3.8) covers a sudden pipe or valve failure and contains no vacancy exclusion. The only peril restricted for unused buildings is vandalism (Article 3.14).
4. Van Loon's statement of 11 September 2026 that the conditions "clearly state" a limited cover and the exclusion of water damage during vacancy is contradicted by the text of those conditions.
5. In July 2026, Van Loon confirmed in writing that other damage remained covered after 3 months of vacancy, and that clause V5069 applies to my unit. That confirmation is consistent with the conditions. It also shows that Van Loon itself reads the vacancy line in opposite ways, which makes the clause ambiguous (see 3.7).

Where the meaning of a policy term is unclear and the insurer drafted it, the interpretation most favourable to the policyholder should prevail (Article 6:238(2) of the Dutch Civil Code, insofar as applicable). I therefore ask Kifid to find that the vacancy limitation cannot be relied on to reject my claim.
