This complaint is not about the technical cause of the water leak. The claim was rejected on one ground only: vacancy. The cause was in any event established contemporaneously by the plumber and reported to Van Loon in time (section 5.5).

### 5.2 The vacancy limitation, as worded, does not clearly apply, and any doubt is resolved in her favour
“Leegstand” is not defined anywhere in the policy documents. A reasonable consumer could understand “empty” or “vacant” as meaning disused or abandoned. This unit had been renovated, was maintained and visited by its owner, and was actively marketed and shown to prospective tenants. Van Loon’s own staff read the clause differently in July 2026. Since the limitation stands in the policy schedule — an individual contractual document — its interpretation is governed by what the parties understood in the light of the whole relationship (the Haviltex standard, Art. 6:248(1) BW); for the VBB 2025-01 conditions, a doubtful clause is construed in favour of the consumer (Art. 6:238(2) BW). At the very least the term is ambiguous, and the ambiguity must be resolved in her favour.

The ambiguity is shown by the Respondents’ own conduct:

### 5.3 Duty of care: the product sold did not fit the risk she declared, and the practical consequence was not explained
An insurance intermediary must inform its client about the product and, when advising, must base its advice on the client’s situation and needs (Art. 4:20 and 4:23 Wft), and must act with the care of a reasonably competent and reasonably acting intermediary (Art. 7:401 BW) [verificar]. That includes promptly alerting the client to the consequences of facts the intermediary knows or should reasonably know, and the duty continues for as long as the policy runs, not only when it is taken out. In a comparable vacancy case the Committee held that a reasonably competent intermediary who knows a property is unoccupied must tell the client that the cover is limited during vacancy (Kifid Geschillencommissie 2024-0504, 18 June 2024, paras 3.3–3.6).

- **Known purpose and known state.** Van Loon knew from its own e-mail of 9 May 2022 that the property was bought “for renting out”, and that it was empty. The quotation was issued on that basis.

- **A product whose extended content is switched off by the very state declared.** She was sold “Gebouw uitgebreid + Glas”, and for four years paid for it, although on the policy schedule that extended content (including water) does not respond for as long as the unit has no tenant. The glass cover (EUR 50.24 per year) is the clearest example of a premium that, while the unit was vacant, added nothing to the fire/storm-only cover [verify the exact wording of the glass cover under vacancy]. On the Respondents’ reading, the owner’s-interest clause (V5069) recommended on 10 May 2022 because “the shop is renovated” is reduced in the same way, although on 9 July 2026 Van Loon confirmed in writing that it applies to her floor and kitchen damage. That recommendation shows that the intermediary understood that what needed protection was the newly renovated interior.

- **The rule was stated, but not explained.** The e-mails of 25 May and 5 August 2022 say in one sentence that cover is “limited to fire and storm” while the property is empty. They do not tell a lay, foreign client that, for as long as she was a landlord looking for a tenant, she would have no cover for water, theft or vandalism; that the product was designed for occupied use; or that other products and measures existed. Her first e-mail (4 May 2022) records a referral from an expat mortgage adviser, which gave the intermediary reason to expect a client unfamiliar with Dutch market practice.

#### (h)(1) Alternatives existed in the market, and none was mentioned.
